GM Food: Facts and Debate

Reading, B1+ · Dietetics and Physical Education · about 40 minutes

1. Key words

Match each word from the texts to its meaning. Then check.

2. Text A: What is a GM food?

Read the text. Then decide if each sentence is true or false.

A genetically modified (GM) food comes from a plant or animal whose DNA was changed in a laboratory. Scientists add a gene from another living thing to give the crop a useful new quality. This is different from traditional breeding, where farmers cross plants for many years and the changes happen more slowly and less precisely.

Today, most GM crops are grown for animal feed, oil and sweeteners. Soybeans, maize, cotton and canola are the most common. A few GM foods are eaten fresh, for example one type of papaya that resists a dangerous virus.

Before a GM food reaches the shops, it must be tested. Experts check if it could cause an allergy, if it contains toxins, and if its nutrients are different from the normal crop. In many countries, government agencies must approve the food first. Major scientific organisations say that the GM foods on sale today are as safe to eat as similar non-GM foods. However, each new product still needs its own safety check.

3. Text B: Should we use GM crops?

Read the two sides of the debate. Then sort the statements.

Supporters say GM crops can help feed a growing population. Some crops give higher yields because they resist insects or disease. One example is Bt maize, which makes its own protection against a pest, so farmers can use less pesticide. GM crops can also be more nutritious. "Golden Rice" was developed to contain vitamin A, because millions of children in some regions do not get enough of it.

Critics say we cannot be sure about the long-term effects on health and nature. Pollen from GM plants can spread to wild plants or to organic fields. Some farmers use a lot of weed-killer on crops designed to survive it, and over time some weeds become resistant. Critics also worry that a few big companies own the seeds, so farmers must buy new ones every year. Many shoppers simply want clear labels so they can choose for themselves.
